\section{Method}

Suppose we observe spike counts $Y$ across a population of $N$ neurons for $T$ time steps, such that

\begin{equation}
Y = \left(\vec{y}_1, \vec{y}_2, \ldots, \vec{y}_T\right),
\end{equation}
where each observation $\vec{y}_t$ is a vector of spike counts across the $N$ neurons,

\begin{equation}
\vec{y}_t =
\begin{bmatrix}
y_t^1 \\
y_t^2 \\
\vdots \\
y_t^N
\end{bmatrix}
\in \mathbb{N}^N.
\end{equation}



We assume that the dynamics of a set of $M$ latent states
\begin{equation}
Q = q_{1}, q_{2},...,q_{M}
\end{equation}
generate the spike counts which are drawn from a Poisson distribution. That is, given the latent state sequence

\begin{equation}
Z = z_{1}, z_{2}, ..., z_{T}
\end{equation}
we have that 

\begin{equation}
B_{n,i} = p(y_{t}^{n} | z_{t} = q_{i}) = \frac{(\lambda_{n,q_{i}})^{y_{t}^{n}} e^{\lambda_{n,q_{i}}}} {y_{t}^{n}!}
\end{equation}
which we call the emissions matrix $B \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times M}$. The dynamics of the latent states are determined probabilisitically, but are assumed to be constrained to ordered sequences such that
\begin{equation}
p(z_{t+1} = q_{i+k} | z_t = q_i) = 0
\end{equation}
except when $k=1$, $i=0$ or when $i+k=1$. This gives us a matrix $T$ with the following form 


\begin{equation}
T =
\begin{bmatrix}
0     & a_{12} & a_{13} & a_{14} & \cdots & a_{1M} \\
a_{21} & 0     & a_{23} & 0      & \cdots & 0 \\
a_{31} & 0     & 0      & a_{34} & \ddots & \vdots \\
a_{41} & 0     & 0      & 0      & \ddots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & a_{M-1,M} \\
a_{M1} & 0     & 0      & 0      & \cdots & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\in \mathbb{R}^{M \times M}.
\end{equation}
Thus, the only allowed transitions are $q_1 \rightarrow q_j$, $q_i \rightarrow q_1$, and $q_i \rightarrow q_{i+1}$.
